Return exactly three tags, ranked from most to least relevant:

- `tag_1`: The primary subfield. This should represent the field that best characterizes the core problem.
- `tag_2`: The most important secondary subfield.
- `tag_3`: A tertiary subfield that is genuinely relevant to solving or understanding the problem.

Use only tags from the taxonomy above. Do not invent new tags.

The three tags must be distinct.

Prefer the **specific physical subfield** over Mathematical Physics when both apply. Use **Mathematical Physics** when specialized mathematical physics concepts or methods are themselves central to solving the problem, rather than simply because the solution involves mathematics.

Likewise, do not use **Experimental & Applied Physics** merely because a question mentions an experiment, instrument, or real-world system. Use it when experimental methodology, instrumentation, measurement, engineering, or applied physical reasoning is substantively required.

For interdisciplinary questions, rank the fields according to their importance to the actual solution. A field that provides the central physical framework should generally rank above a field that provides context or a supporting technique.

The reference answer is provided to help identify the concepts and expertise actually required to solve the problem. **Do not classify based simply on words or topics appearing in the reference answer.** Treat it as additional evidence about the reasoning required.

Even when only one or two subfields are strongly relevant, you must still select the three best-fitting tags. In such cases, rank the weaker association third rather than inventing a category.
